# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

We as members, contributors, maintainers, stewards, and researchers pledge to make participation in the Senvoryn project a **harassment-free experience for everyone**, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, caste, color, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

We also pledge to act in ways that advance the project's actual purpose: **making computing understandable, controllable, and recoverable by the people who own their devices.**

---

## Our standards

### Behavior that builds the project

- **Being precise.** "This is wrong" is not a review; "this violates INV-03 because the child scope exceeds the parent's" is. Disagreement is resolved against the contract, not against the person.
- **Assuming good faith, and saying so.** Most disagreements are misunderstandings, not bad intent.
- **Respecting scope.** A change that weakens an invariant for convenience is a rejection, not a style preference.
- **Disclosing conflicts of interest** when reviewing security-critical changes.
- **Accepting that "proposed" means proposed.** Unproven work is welcome *labeled* as unproven. Claiming more than the evidence supports is the one thing that gets escalated quickly.
- **Giving credit, and taking credit honestly.** Credit the reporter; claim your own work accurately.
- **Being patient with new contributors.** The project is hard. Explaining the reasoning is more valuable than a terse rejection.

### Behavior that is unacceptable

- Harassment, intimidation, or discrimination in any form.
- Sexualized language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention or advances.
- Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks.
- Public or private harassment.
- Publishing others' private information — a physical or email address, or any identifying information — without explicit permission.
- Sustained disruption of discussions, or pressuring others into abandoning a technical position.
- **Deliberately misleading claims about the project's capabilities**, in issues, PRs, comments, or marketing.
- **Publishing a vulnerability before a fix is available**, except where the reporter is being harmed by delay and has given clear notice — see [`SECURITY.md`](SECURITY.md).
- Other conduct which could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting.

### Conduct in technical disagreement

Senvoryn's design has a specific character: **authority must be explicit and verifiable.** Technical disagreements are expected to be sharp. What is not acceptable is:

- **Attacking the person instead of the argument.** "This reasoning is wrong" — yes. "You clearly don't understand security" — no.
- **Weaponizing the constitution to win a design debate.** Invariants constrain the project; they are not a trump card for a preferred architecture. If an invariant genuinely blocks your proposal, propose an amendment through the process in [`GOVERNANCE.md`](GOVERNANCE.md) — that is the designed path, and it works.
- **Downgrading someone's contribution because it is not finished.** Work in progress is welcome; say what state it is in.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies within all project spaces — the repository, issues, pull requests, discussions, advisories, mailing lists, chat channels, and any release or event associated with the project. It also applies when an individual officially represents the project in public spaces.

---

## Enforcement

### Reporting

Instances of abusive, harassing, or otherwise unacceptable behavior may be reported to the project maintainers through **GitHub's private reporting** on this repository, or by opening a confidential report through GitHub's *"Report abuse"* feature on any comment or issue.

All complaints will be **reviewed and investigated promptly and fairly**. The project will respect the privacy and security of the reporter of any incident.

### Enforcement guidelines

Community leaders and maintainers are responsible for clarifying and enforcing these standards and will take appropriate and fair corrective action in response to any behavior they deem inappropriate, threatening, offensive, or harmful.

**Severity ladder:**

| Level | Response |
|---|---|
| **1 — Correction** | A private, written warning, with clarity about the nature of the violation and why the behavior was inappropriate. A public apology may be requested. |
| **2 — Warning** | A warning with consequences for continued behavior. No interaction with the people involved for a specified period. Violating these terms may lead to a temporary or permanent ban. |
| **3 — Temporary ban** | A temporary ban from any sort of interaction or public communication with the project for a specified period. Violating these terms may lead to a permanent ban. |
| **4 — Permanent ban** | A permanent ban from any sort of public interaction within the project. |

### Scope of sanctions

- **Permanent ban** — for sustained inappropriate behavior, harassment, aggression toward or disparagement of classes of individuals, or sustained disregard for the project's stated principles.
- **Temporary ban** — for sustained or serious inappropriate behavior, including sustained disruption of project activities.
- **Correction / warning** — for a first or minor infraction, or for behavior that a maintainer believes was accidental or misunderstood.

### Attribution

Maintainers will not name a reporter publicly beyond what the reporter has chosen to disclose, and will not name a violator where the violation is not publicly relevant.

---

## Scope, continued

This policy applies to **all** contributors equally. Maintainers and stewards are held to a higher standard, not a lower one. Maintainers who fail to enforce this policy may face temporary or permanent removal from project roles.
